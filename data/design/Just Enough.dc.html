The finding

A flat 95% service level costs 2.5× more than doing nothing sophisticated at all.

Understocking costs you the margin. Overstocking a perishable costs close to the whole unit. With a 30% margin and 60% spoilage the critical ratio lands at 0.409 — so the cost-optimal service level is 41%, and every unit you stock past that is spoilage you paid for in advance.

The winning policy accepts a higher stockout rate than doing nothing and still costs 28.6% less, because it cuts waste by 73%. On thin-margin perishables an empty shelf is cheaper than a full bin.

How it works

Three steps between a CSV and an order sheet.

STEP 01

Upload sales history

Drop a CSV of SKU, store, date and units. A global gradient-boosted model fits the full demand distribution — seven quantile levels, not a single point forecast.

STEP 02

State your two costs

What a missed sale costs, and what a spoiled unit costs. Those two numbers set the critical ratio, and the critical ratio is the whole policy.

STEP 03

Read the order sheet

Per-SKU quantities, buffer stock and expected cost, priced against the naive and fixed-95% alternatives so the saving is auditable rather than asserted.
